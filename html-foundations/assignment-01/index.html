Classic Smoky Nigerian Jollof Rice

Few dishes bring people together quite like a steaming pot of Nigerian Jollof rice. Known for its rich tomato base, warm spices, and signature smoky aroma, this recipe walks you through making party-style Jollof right in your own kitchen.

Kitchen Safety First

Warning: Hot oil and blended tomato sauce can splatter violently when they hit a hot pan. Always keep a pot lid nearby to shield yourself, and never leave a pot of simmering rice unattended on high heat, as the bottom can burn quickly and fill your kitchen with smoke.

Ingredients

  • 3 cups of long-grain parboiled rice
  • 6 large ripe Roma tomatoes
  • 3 red bell peppers (tatashe)
  • 2 Scotch bonnet peppers (ata rodo), adjusted to your heat tolerance
  • 2 medium red onions (one for blending, one sliced)
  • 3 tablespoons of double-concentrated tomato paste
  • 2 cups of rich chicken or vegetable stock
  • 1/2 cup of vegetable oil
  • 2 teaspoons of curry powder
  • 1 teaspoon of dried thyme
  • 2 bay leaves
  • Salt and seasoning cubes to taste

Preparation Steps

  1. Blend the Roma tomatoes, red bell peppers, Scotch bonnet peppers, and one red onion with a splash of water until completely smooth.
  2. Pour the blended mixture into a pot and boil it over medium heat for about 15 minutes until the excess water evaporates and the puree thickens.
  3. Wash the parboiled rice thoroughly in cold water until the water runs clear, then drain it in a colander.
  4. Heat the vegetable oil in a large, heavy-bottomed pot and fry the sliced onion for 2 minutes until fragrant.
  5. Stir in the tomato paste and fry for 5 minutes, stirring constantly so it does not stick.
  6. Pour in the reduced tomato-pepper puree, curry powder, thyme, bay leaves, and seasoning cubes, then let the sauce fry for 10 minutes until the oil rises to the top.
  7. Pour in the chicken stock and stir well, then add the washed rice so the liquid sits just level with the top of the grains.
  8. Cover the pot tightly with foil and a lid to trap the steam, then cook on low heat for 25 to 30 minutes without lifting the lid too often.
  9. Once the rice is tender and the liquid is absorbed, turn up the heat for 60 seconds to create that signature smoky bottom layer, then fluff with a fork and serve.

Helpful Cooking Terms

Parboiled Rice
Rice that has been partially boiled in its husk before milling, which helps the grains stay firm, separate, and fluffy instead of turning mushy.
Simmer
To cook liquid gently over low heat just below the boiling point, allowing tiny bubbles to rise slowly to the surface.
Reduce
To boil a liquid or sauce uncovered so excess water evaporates, leaving behind a thicker texture and deeper flavor.
De-glaze
Adding liquid like stock or water to a hot pan to loosen the flavorful browned bits stuck to the bottom.
